What is an occupational medical?

An occupational medical is a health assessment done for work purposes. Its job is not to diagnose or treat illness. It checks whether a person is fit to do a specific job safely, without putting their own health or the safety of the people around them at risk.

Someone who is healthy in daily life can still face real risks at work: climbing at height, operating heavy machinery, spending hours in loud environments or handling food. A medical looks at the health factors that matter for that kind of work.

Medicals are normally arranged by the employer, or required by a client or site before a worker may start. Which medicals you need, and how often, depends on the job, the industry and the rules that apply to your workplace.

Medicals for every type of work

Choose the certificate your site or employer requires. Not sure which one applies? Ask us and we’ll point you to the right one.

  • General medical

    A baseline assessment of overall health for new hires, routine screening and returning staff. It records a worker’s health at a point in time, so later check-ups have something to compare against.

  • Working at heights

    For anyone who works above ground level, such as scaffolders, riggers, roofers and maintenance teams. The assessment looks at factors that could affect safety at height, such as dizziness, blackouts, vision and general fitness.

  • Operator medical

    For people who drive or operate machines, plant and vehicles. Vision, hearing and anything that could affect alertness or reaction time matter most here, because an operator’s condition affects the safety of others.

  • Food handling

    For people who prepare, pack or serve food. The aim is to confirm there is no condition that could put food safety, and the people eating it, at risk.

  • Exit medical

    A health record taken when employment ends. It documents the worker’s condition on leaving, which can matter if a health question comes up later.

Inside a medical

The tests carried out depend on the type of medical and what your employer or site requires.

Identity check

We confirm who is being assessed using their ID or passport number, plus an ID photo and a face photo, so the results belong to the right person.

Nurse examination

A general physical check, including health history and basic readings such as height, weight and blood pressure.

Vision test

Checks eyesight against what the job needs, for example when operating equipment or working at height.

Hearing test

Audiometry measures hearing, which is especially important for workers exposed to noise.

Lung function

Spirometry measures how well the lungs work, which matters for dusty, dirty or physically demanding work.

Understanding the result

At the end of a medical, the findings are put together and the worker’s fitness for the job is recorded. Typical outcomes are:

Fit

No health concern that stops the person doing the job safely.

Fit with restrictions

Able to work, with conditions, such as not working at heights or needing corrective glasses.

Temporarily unfit

Not fit right now, but may be after treatment or a follow-up with their own doctor.

Unfit

A health issue makes this particular job unsafe for the person or for others.

A result applies to a specific job. Someone who is unfit for one role may well be fit for another. The final result is finalised and sent to you as a PDF.

Getting ready for your medical

A little preparation helps things go smoothly.

  • Bring your ID or passport
  • Bring your glasses or contact lenses
  • Bring hearing aids if you use them
  • Bring a list of any medication you take
  • Bring earlier medical records or certificates
  • Bring any letter or job details from your employer
  • Avoid loud noise before a hearing test where you can
  • Be rested and eat as normal

Common questions

Quick answers about how medicals work.

Which medical do I need?

It depends on the work being done. If the job involves heights, operating machines or handling food, one of those specific medicals usually applies. If you’re not sure, ask your employer or send us the job details and we’ll point you to the right one.

How long does a medical take?

That depends on the type and the tests required. Ask us when you book and we’ll give you a realistic idea for your group.

Who arranges and pays for it?

Usually the employer. A company name is needed to book a medical.

How often must a medical be repeated?

It depends on the job, the industry and the rules of your workplace or site. Many employers repeat medicals regularly. Check with your employer or ask us.

Is my personal information kept private?

Yes. Personal and health information is treated as confidential and is only used to process the medical and share the result with the people who need it.

What happens if I’m found unfit or have restrictions?

It isn’t the end of the road. Restrictions may allow you to keep working safely, and a temporary result may change after treatment or a follow-up with your own doctor.
